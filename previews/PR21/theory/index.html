Theory

This page states the optimization problems behind L2ODLL, the dual completions it computes in closed form, and their gradients. References:

  • [DLL] M. Tanneau and P. Van Hentenryck, Dual Lagrangian Learning for Conic Optimization, NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2402.03086.
  • [DIPOL] M. Klamkin, M. Tanneau and P. Van Hentenryck, Dual Interior Point Optimization Learning, arXiv:2402.02596.

Equation numbers such as "(DLL 9)" refer to the arXiv versions of these papers.

Notation

symbolmeaning
$p \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$parameters (JuMP MOI.Parameter variables); the input of the proxy
$x \in \mathbb{R}^n$primal decision variables
$\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{K}$closed convex cones (products of MOI sets: $\mathbb{R}_+$, $\{0\}$, second-order, PSD, …)
$\mathcal{C}^*$dual cone, $\{y : y^\top s \ge 0\ \forall s \in \mathcal{C}\}$ (DLL 2)
$y$predicted dual variables (one block per constraint in get_y)
$z$completed dual variables (one block per constraint in get_z)
$w$quadratic slack variables of the dual (convex quadratic objectives)
$\Pi_{\mathcal{C}^*}$Euclidean projection onto $\mathcal{C}^*$ (DLL 1)
$\lvert v\rvert^+ = \max(v, 0)$, $\lvert v\rvert^- = \max(-v, 0)$elementwise

Sign convention. L2ODLL follows MathOptInterface: a constraint is written as "function in set", $Ax + b \in \mathcal{C}$, and its dual is $y \in \mathcal{C}^*$ with the signs returned by JuMP.dual. The papers write $Ax \succeq_{\mathcal{K}} b$; the two forms coincide with $b \mapsto -b$. Maximization problems are handled by Dualization.jl, so the predicted $y$ always has JuMP.dual signs.

Parametric primal–dual pair

For fixed parameters $p$ consider the conic problem and its dual (DLL 4–5):

\[\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} & \min\nolimits_{x} & c(p)^\top x \\ & \;\;\text{s.t.} & Ax + b(p) \in \mathcal{C} \end{aligned} \quad\quad\quad\quad \begin{aligned} & \max\nolimits_{y} & - b(p)^\top y \\ & \;\;\text{s.t.} & A^\top y = c(p) \\ & & y \in \mathcal{C}^* \end{aligned} \tag{1} \end{equation}\]

Parametrization. The parameters enter affinely in the data $b(p)$ and $c(p)$ (constraint constants and linear objective coefficients, e.g. @objective(m, Max, μ' * x) with parameters μ). The constraint matrix $A$ and the quadratic objective $Q$ do not depend on $p$; a parameter multiplying a variable in a constraint is rejected with an error.

Weak duality. For every $p$ and every dual-feasible $y$ ($A^\top y = c(p)$, $y \in \mathcal{C}^*$), $-b(p)^\top y \le$ the optimal value of the primal. No convexity of the parameter dependence and no constraint qualification is needed. Strong duality (equality at the optimum) holds under Slater's condition.

Decomposition and completion

Split the constraints into a predicted block (cone $\mathcal{C}$, duals $y$) and a completed block $Hx + h(p) \in \mathcal{K}$ (duals $z$) (DLL 6–7):

\[\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} & \max\nolimits_{y,z} & - b(p)^\top y - h(p)^\top z \\ & \;\;\text{s.t.} & A^\top y + H^\top z = c(p) \\ & & y \in \mathcal{C}^*,\; z \in \mathcal{K}^* \end{aligned} \tag{2} \end{equation}\]

Given a prediction $\hat y$, DLL projects it, $\bar y = \Pi_{\mathcal{C}^*}(\hat y)$, and completes $z$ by solving (DLL 9), whose dual is (DLL 8):

\[\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \Phi(\bar y, p) = & \max\nolimits_{z} & - h(p)^\top z - b(p)^\top \bar y \\ & \;\;\text{s.t.} & H^\top z = c(p) - A^\top \bar y \\ & & z \in \mathcal{K}^* \end{aligned} \quad\quad\quad\quad \begin{aligned} & \min\nolimits_{x} & (c(p) - A^\top \bar y)^\top x - b(p)^\top \bar y \\ & \;\;\text{s.t.} & Hx + h(p) \in \mathcal{K} \end{aligned} \tag{3} \end{equation}\]

dual_objective returns $\Phi(\bar y, p)$; dual_completion returns $(\bar y, z, w)$.

Theorem (DLL Theorem 1, dual conic completion). Assume that for every ``\bar y \in \mathcal{C}^$the right-hand problem in (3) is bounded and strictly feasible. Then the left-hand problem in (3) has an optimal$z$, and$(\bar y, z)`` is feasible for (2).*

Theorem (DLL Theorem 2, optimal completion). Under the same assumptions, $\Phi(\bar y, p)$ is a valid bound on the primal optimum, and it is the best bound among all dual-feasible points whose $y$-block equals $\bar y$.

In particular $\Phi(y^\star, p)$ equals the optimal value when $y^\star$ is the $y$-block of an optimal dual solution.

Closed-form completions

The hypothesis of Theorem 1 holds, and (3) has a closed form, in two structured cases that L2ODLL detects automatically (decomposition detection).

Bounded variables (DLL Example 1, eqs. 11–18; BoundDecomposition). If $l \le x \le u$ with finite $l < u$ and the objective is affine in $x$, take the bounds as the completed block. With $r = c(p) - A^\top \bar y$,

\[z_l = \lvert r\rvert^+, \qquad z_u = -\lvert r\rvert^-, \qquad \Phi(\bar y, p) = -b(p)^\top \bar y + l^\top z_l + u^\top z_u . \tag{4}\]

(L2ODLL keeps $z_u \le 0$, the MOI sign of the dual of $x \le u$; DLL writes $z^u = \lvert r\rvert^- \ge 0$ with a minus sign in the objective.)

Log-barrier completion (DIPOL Theorem 2, eq. 7). Replacing the completion objective by $-h^\top z + \mu \sum_j (\ln z_{l,j} + \ln(-z_{u,j}))$ gives, with $v = \mu / (u - l)$ and $\omega = r/2$,

\[z_l = v + \omega + \sqrt{v^2 + \omega^2}, \qquad z_u = -v + \omega - \sqrt{v^2 + \omega^2}. \tag{5}\]

It still satisfies $z_l + z_u = r$ with $z_l > 0 > z_u$, so the bound (4) evaluated at (5) is valid, and it tends to (4) as $\mu \to 0$; it is smooth in $\bar y$. It is available as L2ODLL.LogBoundedCompletion in L2ODLL.bounded_builder(...; completion = :log, μ).

Strictly convex quadratic objective (DLL Example 3, eqs. 21–23; ConvexQP). For $\min \tfrac12 x^\top Q x + c(p)^\top x$ with $Q \succ 0$ the dual has a free block $w$ with stationarity $A^\top y - Q w = c(p)$ (the sign convention of Dualization.jl). Predicting all constraint duals and completing $w$,

\[w = Q^{-1}\big(A^\top \bar y - c(p)\big), \qquad \Phi(\bar y, p) = -b(p)^\top \bar y - \tfrac12 w^\top Q w . \tag{6}\]

$w$ is the minimizer of the Lagrangian, $x(\bar y)$; at an optimal $y^\star$ it is the (unique) primal optimum. (The code reads the coefficient matrix of $w$ from the dual written by Dualization.jl, so the $\tfrac12$ convention of the model does not matter.)

Projection

The projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}^*}$ is computed per constraint with MathOptSetDistances.jl (Euclidean projection, DLL eq. 1): max(y, 0) for $\mathbb{R}_+$, the identity for equalities, the closed-form projection for second-order cones, eigenvalue clipping for PSD cones. DLL also proposes a radial projection (DLL 26) that avoids zero gradients on the polar cone; any map into $\mathcal{C}^*$ keeps the bound valid, and a custom one can be passed as proj_fn to decompose! (see the package guide).

Gradient of the completed dual objective

Training needs $\nabla_{\hat y}\,\Phi(\Pi_{\mathcal{C}^*}(\hat y), p)$.

Theorem (DIPOL Theorem 3). For the bounded completion (4)–(5) with $\mu \ge 0$, $\Phi$ is concave in $\bar y$ and $-b(p) - A\hat x$ is a supergradient, where $\hat x$ is an optimal dual solution of the equality constraints in (3) (the right-hand problem). For $\mu > 0$ it is differentiable.

Theorem (DIPOL Theorem 4). For $\mu = 0$, $\hat x_j = l_j$ if $r_j > 0$, $\hat x_j = u_j$ if $r_j < 0$ and any $\hat x_j \in [l_j, u_j]$ if $r_j = 0$.

In the convention of the papers ($Ax \ge b$) this reads $b - A\hat x$. The same argument (partial Lagrangian of (3)) gives $-b(p) - A\hat x$ for the generic completion, with $\hat x$ the dual of the equality constraints of the completion problem (issue #5). When the network also predicts a quadratic slack $w$ (bounded decomposition with a quadratic objective, not implemented yet) its gradient with respect to $w$ is $Q(\hat x - w)$ for the objective $\tfrac12 x^\top Q x$ (see decomposition detection).

L2ODLL differentiates the closed forms (4) and (6) with automatic differentiation (ForwardDiff by default via DifferentiationInterface; Zygote also works), which yields the same (super)gradient almost everywhere.

Training problem

The proxy $\hat y = f_\theta(p)$ is trained self-supervised by maximizing the expected bound (DLL 27a; DIPOL 3):

\[\max_\theta\; \mathbb{E}_{p}\big[\Phi(\Pi_{\mathcal{C}^*}(f_\theta(p)), p)\big]. \tag{7}\]

No optimal solutions are needed. For a maximization primal the dual is a minimization and (7) becomes a minimization. In the L2OCore interface the loss is always minimized, so it is $-\text{mean}\,\Phi$ for minimization problems and $+\text{mean}\,\Phi$ for maximization problems.

Deviations from the papers

  • Signs. MOI convention throughout (see above); both forms define the same bound.
  • Projection. Euclidean projection by default; DLL's radial projection is available for second-order cones with projection = :radial.
  • Bounded decomposition with a quadratic objective. The background pages describe predicting $(y, w)$ and completing the bounds; this is not implemented yet. Such models are detected as ConvexQP (if the objective is strictly convex), and requesting BoundDecomposition explicitly raises an error.
  • Log-barrier layer. With completion = :log, the layer returns the plain dual objective (4) evaluated at the smoothed completion (5): a valid bound, smooth in $\bar y$. DIPOL trains with the barrier-augmented objective (DIPOL eq. 4), whose supergradient is $-b - A x_\mu$ (Theorems 3, 5); the plain objective has a different gradient. The barrier-augmented value can be built from L2ODLL.complete_zlzu, which returns $(z_l, z_u)$ of (5).
  • Infeasible generic completions. Only the two closed forms satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem 1 by construction. A GenericDecomposition completion may be infeasible (issue #6); the JuMP layer then throws instead of returning a bound.
